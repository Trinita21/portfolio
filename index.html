Coaching that knows you, in a room that feels real

Corporate training is generic, and generic training is forgotten. The goal is an LLM-driven coach that lives inside immersive environments and responds to context, emotion and the learner's own work.

Problem

Training platforms don't adapt to a person's context, emotional state or enterprise setting, so coaching stays shallow and one-size-fits-all.

Approach

  • Immersive, LLM-driven coaching platform with multimodal interaction (speech, text and scene).
  • Integration with enterprise data so feedback is grounded in the learner's real context.
  • Context-aware, emotionally responsive behaviour, designed on top of the trust and privacy foundations of my earlier work.

Status

Research in progress. Findings will appear here and in Writing as they're published.

Turning an attack into a privacy switch

Adversarial audio is usually studied as a threat to defend against. I asked what happens if you use it as a precision control inside the model instead.

utterance-level redaction
97.7%

99.1%

Problem

ASR transcribes everything it hears, including passwords, medical details and names. Post-hoc redaction is brittle, and it comes too late.

Approach

Protective Acoustic Triggering (PAT). Fine-tuned Whisper variants so a designed acoustic trigger activates privacy-preserving behaviour inside the model. When triggered, the ASR performs surgical phrase-level masking of the sensitive span and leaves the rest of the transcript intact.

Result

Near-baseline transcription accuracy with very high redaction success, a new family of privacy-aware ASR models.

Closing the temporal coupling trust gap

Users can't say "stop listening" mid-sentence, and can't prove afterwards that anything was forgotten. This thesis builds both abilities into a conversational system.

Every privacy decision the system makes is a record you can verify, not a promise you have to believe.

Problem

Modern speech and chat AI couples what it hears with what it keeps. Privacy controls are static settings, not something you can invoke in the moment, and nothing is auditable.

Approach

  • Three pillars: trigger detection, policy enforcement and memory management, for both chat and speech.
  • Acoustic and textual triggers, detected by fine-tuned RoBERTa and WavLM, let users pause, mask or delete mid-stream.
  • A verifiable state machine enforces policy, with a cryptographic hash chain for tamper-evident audit logs.
  • A deletion system recomputes embeddings to eliminate "ghost influence", the lingering traces of deleted data.

Evidence

Validated with membership-inference and semantic-leakage tests, then evaluated end to end on chat and meeting scenarios. Strong privacy–utility trade-offs with minimal utility loss.

To defend speech models you first have to know how they break. I designed a universal trigger that takes control of Whisper's output.

0.75s
adversarial segment
97%
of speech samples affected

Problem

Large speech models are trusted in production but vulnerable to gradient-based attacks, and testing them at scale is slow and ad hoc.

Approach

  • A universal acoustic trigger that directs specific token outputs.
  • Black-box and white-box attack frameworks with automated testing for scalable LLM evaluation.

Result

Strong cross-dataset transferability, exposing real vulnerabilities in Whisper. This work later became the seed of PAT.
